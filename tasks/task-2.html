Task 2: The Original Isolation Forest Paper

Read the paper that started it all, and understand the core idea behind Isolation Forest.

Key Points

Reading

Start reading the original paper: Liu, Ting & Zhou (2008), "Isolation Forest". The paper introduces Isolation Forest, which isolates anomalies via random recursive partitioning instead of profiling normal points.

Assignment 2: Annotate the Paper

This paper is short, but it can be dense if you've never read a research paper before. Read it slowly, and don't worry about following every equation on the first pass. Read and highlight or annotate the PDF as you go, marking whatever you find important, confusing, or worth coming back to. A few things to look for as you read:

  • What two properties do anomalies have that Isolation Forest relies on? (Hint: the paper describes them as "few" and "different.")
  • What is an isolation tree, and how is one built?
  • Why do anomalies tend to get isolated in fewer random splits than normal points?
  • Without deriving the full formula, what does an anomaly score close to 1 mean? What about a score close to 0.5?
  • What problems do distance-based or density-based anomaly detection methods run into that Isolation Forest avoids?
  • Why is Isolation Forest fast and memory-efficient compared to those other methods?

Once you're done, upload your annotated PDF to the shared Google Drive folder.
