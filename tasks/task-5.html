Task 5: Local Outlier Factor

Read the paper that introduces LOF, then baseline it on the same data as Isolation Forest.

Key Points

Reading

Start reading the original paper: Breunig, Kriegel, Ng & Sander (2000), "LOF: Identifying Density-Based Local Outliers". Instead of isolating points with random splits, LOF scores each point by comparing its local density to the density of its neighbors, a point in a notably sparser neighborhood than its neighbors gets a high score.

Implementation

Using scikit-learn's LocalOutlierFactor, fit a baseline model with default parameters on the same features used for the Isolation Forest baseline in Task 3. Labels withheld during fit, same as before.

Assignment 5, Part 1: Annotate the Paper (Scoped Reading)

This paper is more math-heavy than the Isolation Forest one. Read in this order and it'll make sense much faster:

  1. The abstract, the introduction, and the motivating two-cluster figure (it's early in the introduction, a dense cluster, a sparse cluster, and one point just outside the dense cluster). That figure is the whole intuition in one picture, find it before you read anything else.
  2. Section 3 (definitions). Read the definitions themselves, skip the lemmas and proofs, they're not what you need here.
  3. A short look at the real-data results section, just enough to see what LOF caught that other methods missed.

Highlight or annotate the PDF as you go, marking whatever you find important, confusing, or worth coming back to. Upload your annotated PDF to the shared Drive folder when done.

Five definitions, in order, that Section 3 is building toward:

  • k-distance of a point: the distance to its k-th nearest neighbor.
  • k-neighborhood: the points within that distance.
  • Reachability distance: the distance from A to B, but never smaller than B's k-distance. This smooths out noise for points that sit very close together.
  • Local reachability density (LRD): the inverse of the average reachability distance from a point to its neighbors. High density means close neighbors.
  • LOF: the average ratio of the neighbors' LRD to the point's own LRD.

Plain-language intuition: LOF asks "is this point's neighborhood much sparser than the neighborhoods of the points around it?" A score near 1 means about as dense as its neighbors, an inlier. A score well above 1 means much sparser than its neighbors, a local outlier.

The word "local" is the whole point. A point in a sparse cluster isn't an outlier just because its cluster is sparse, that's exactly what a single global density threshold would get wrong, and exactly what the two-cluster figure is showing you. For the fraud angle: a transaction that's normal for one customer segment may be suspicious for another, same local-vs-global distinction.

Optional, but doing it once makes the definitions stick: hand-compute LOF for 6-8 points laid out on a 1D number line. It's a few minutes of arithmetic and the five definitions stop being abstract.

Assignment 5, Part 2: Build the Baseline on Real Data

Fit the default-parameter LocalOutlierFactor on the same data as the Isolation Forest baseline and plot the score distribution, then sanity-check against labels across both the top 10 and the top 3.5% by score. With 401 features, fitting the full dataset can take around 2 hours, start with a 20,000-30,000 row subsample instead.
